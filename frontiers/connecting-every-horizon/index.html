Connecting every horizon

Transmission lines that reach every community, and why that is now a matter of national security.

By The Next 25Feature · 6 min readThe electricity era · 2026
Scroll to read

Generating clean energy is half the battle. Renewables and megacities demand a radically reimagined grid network: a new energy highway, built across every geography, reaching every horizon. And in the last few years, something changed. The wire stopped being just infrastructure. It became strategy.

The new frontline

The IEA calls it the Age of Electricity. In 2024, electricity demand grew roughly twice as fast as total energy use, and it is forecast to keep growing at 3.6% a year through 2030. Electricity is only about 21% of final energy consumption, yet it underpins more than 40% of the global economy. What was once an oil-and-gas question has become a power-grid question, and electricity security has become a central pillar of national security.

Unlike fuels, electricity cannot be stored in tanks or rerouted on ships. Supply and demand balance in real time, which means a disruption anywhere in the chain, from generation to grids to digital controls, can trigger an immediate, economy-wide shock.

Three threats, one grid

The paper behind this story names three escalating external threats. Supply chains for transformers, high-voltage equipment and cables are concentrated, delayed and increasingly politicized. Attackers now blend the physical and the digital: a 2025 cyberattack on Poland's electricity infrastructure targeted more than 30 renewable plants and nearly triggered a blackout, and coordinated arson struck Berlin's cable bridges in 2026. And climate-driven hazards keep outpacing infrastructure built for a milder past, as Winter Storm Uri showed the United States at a cost of tens of billions.

Resilience is a wire

A community with a strong grid connection has options: industry, education, healthcare, growth. A community without one waits. That is why the response is measured in build, not in words. Over $1 trillion in grid investment is needed to connect Europe's clean energy future alone. More than $9 billion invested in capacity and people. One in four high-voltage switchgear installed worldwide, an installed base in more than 140 countries, and the lead in SF6-free technology.

The paper sets out six moves that turn resilience from a slogan into a plan: longer-term coordinated system planning, system-wide flexibility, diversified and secured supply chains, strategic reserves of critical technologies, accelerated digitalization and situational awareness, and the skilled workforce to run it all. Countries that act now will protect their citizens and lead the next decade of industrial competition. Countries that delay will pay in jobs, growth and strategic autonomy.
