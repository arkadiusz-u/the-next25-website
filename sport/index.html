Ninety minutes, zero margin - and a million kettles

Sport is the last thing a whole country does at the same second.

The Next 25TopicThe electricity era · 2026
Scroll to read

In Britain the grid control room plans for a phenomenon it calls TV pickup. At the final whistle a large part of the country stands up and puts the kettle on, and demand jumps by hundreds of megawatts in about a minute. The record still belongs to football: roughly 2,800 megawatts at the end of the 1990 World Cup semi-final penalty shootout. The control room knew it was coming, and had the generation waiting.

Sport is also one of the most visible casualties of a warming planet. Of 93 potential Winter Olympic host locations, only 52 will still have reliable conditions by the 2050s. For the Paralympic Winter Games, held in March, the figure falls to 22.

The answer is for venues to stop consuming power and start supporting the system - and for the sport itself to reach people no energy company reaches.
