Ain't no mountain high enough

How power reaches the places the map forgets. The best wind sits in the worst places, and getting it to a city is the real feat.

By The Next 25Feature · 4 min readThe electricity era · 2026
Scroll to read

Clean energy requires wind and solar at scale, and the most potent sources sit in the most remote, hostile environments on earth. The North Sea does not care about anyone's climate targets. Harnessing it is an engineering feat of the first order.

That is what the ultimate energy bridge does. More than 175 GW of HVDC integrated globally, enough to power two thirds of European households, built with partners like TenneT, Ørsted, Amprion and SSEN. From the roughest oceans to urban grids, it means mastering nature's volatility so millions receive reliable, sustainable electricity.

The elements deliver, the grid decides

Renewables are no longer the experiment; they are the plan. What decides whether the plan works is transmission: the links that carry offshore wind ashore, steady the grid when the wind drops, and turn distant sunlight into a city's evening.

The clean power we already waste

Here is the frustration at the heart of the transition. More than 1,700 GW of renewable projects sit in European connection queues, waiting for a grid that has not been built yet. And of the clean power that does get generated, a growing share is curtailed: switched off because the network cannot carry it. In just seven European countries in one year, roughly €7.2 billion of renewable electricity was curtailed.

Fixing it pays. Deeper market integration could save up to €40 billion a year, and a 50% increase in cross-border electricity trade could lift EU GDP measurably. The wind is free. The wire is the investment.
