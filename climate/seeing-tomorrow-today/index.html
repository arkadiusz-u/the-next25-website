Seeing tomorrow today

Step into the digital control room, where failures are predicted before they happen.

By The Next 25Feature · 4 min readThe electricity era · 2026
Scroll to read

The energy transition is too complex for legacy systems. Operators need investment-grade data and predictive modelling, because the grid of the electricity era cannot afford surprises.

That is what AI-powered grid management delivers: more than 175 physics-based and machine-learning models detecting asset degradation and predicting failures before they occur. Digital twins that eliminate weeks of preparation. Enterprise software, SCADA and energy simulations that accelerate decisions, wrapped in cybersecurity.

Clairvoyance through code

This is not vague digital transformation. It is a specific set of tools that lets an operator see a transformer weakening months before it fails, and act on a Tuesday instead of during a blackout. The grid that sees tomorrow keeps the lights on today.

Complexity is the new normal

The reason the control room had to go digital is simple: the system it manages changed shape. Wind and solar overtook fossil power in the EU, output now swings across hours, days and seasons, and cross-border flows have multiplied. Grid spend doubled in a decade to around €70 billion a year in Europe, and it is still short of what the build requires.

You cannot run that system on yesterday’s tools. Which is why clairvoyance through code stops being a phrase and becomes an operating requirement: see the weakness, schedule the fix, keep the lights on.
